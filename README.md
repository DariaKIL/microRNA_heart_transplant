## Abstract

> **Background:** Heart transplantation (HT) remains the definitive treatment for patients with end-stage heart failure. However, long-term outcomes are compromised by acute cellular rejection (ACR), antibody-mediated rejection (AMR), and cardiac allograft vasculopathy (CAV), a chronic form of transplant vasculopathy. Current post-transplant allograft surveillance relies primarily on endomyocardial biopsy and coronary angiography, both of which are invasive, associated with procedural risks, costly, and complicated for frequent monitoring. Circulating miRNAs have emerged as promising liquid biopsy candidate markers for the non-invasive assessment of transplant injury. Nevertheless, reliable molecular markers capable of discriminating between the distinct pathological phenotypes of rejection and graft injury remain unavailable.  
> **Aim:** To evaluate circulating miRNA profiles as exploratory candidate markers for detecting rejection in heart transplant recipients within the first five years post-transplantation.  
> **Methods:** Plasma samples were collected from 20 heart transplant (HT) recipients, including three with ACR (R1/Grade 2), four with severe AMR (pAMR2, pAMR2/3, or pAMR3), five with CAV, and eight without rejection (NR). Circulating miRNA profiles were generated by next-generation sequencing using the TruSeq Small RNA Library Prep Kit (Illumina, USA). Differential expression and bioinformatic analysis, including functional enrichment, were performed using miRge3.0, DESeq2, miRBaseConverter, and clusterProfiler. Statistical analyses were conducted in R (version 4.4.2).  
> **Results:** After filtering low-abundance transcripts, a total of 642 miRNAs were detected across all samples. Following apeglm shrinkage, hsa-miR-193b-5p, hsa-miR-125b-5p, and hsa-miR-5588-5p were significantly upregulated in AMR group compared to NR, CAV, and ACR groups (padj < 0.05). No significant differentially expressed miRNAs were identified between ACR and CAV, ACR and NR, or CAV and NR groups after shrinkage correction. Notably, hsa-miR-193b-5p retained a significant association with AMR after exploratory adjustment for contemporaneous anti-HLA antibody status, demonstrating a large effect size (shrunken log2 fold change >2.5). Differentially expressed miRNAs targeted biological processes related to cellular stress responses, cell adhesion, immune activation, and tissue remodeling.  
> **Conclusion:** Our study highlights hsa-miR-193b-5p, hsa-miR-125b-5p, and hsa-miR-5588-5p as exploratory candidate biomarkers associated with AMR. Validation in larger cohorts is required to establish their potential to distinguish AMR from stable graft function and other forms of cardiac allograft injury.

## Analysis Pipeline
**1. Differential Expression Analysis (microRNA_transplant.Rmd)**  

This notebook covers the primary processing of the small RNA-seq data:

*Quality Control & Exploratory Data Analysis:* RNA class distribution (Figure 1A) and PCA/Sample distance heatmap.  
*DESeq2 Modeling:* Pairwise comparisons (e.g., AMR vs. NR) with apeglm LFC shrinkage.  
*Covariate Adjustments:* Models adjusted for HLA sensitization status (~ antibody + condition).  
*Multi-group Analysis (LRT):* Likelihood Ratio Test to identify miRNAs with significant changes across any clinical condition.   
*Visualizations:* Volcano plots, Venn diagrams (transcriptome intersections), and normalized expression boxplots.

**2. Target Prediction & Enrichment (microRNA_transplant_enrich.Rmd)**  

This notebook explores the biological role of the identified candidate miRNAs:  

*In Silico Target Prediction:* Retrieval of experimentally validated targets from miRTarBase via multiMiR.  
*Functional Enrichment:* GO (Biological Process) and Reactome pathway analysis using clusterProfiler.  
*Network Analysis:* Construction of miRNA-mRNA interaction networks identifying central "hub" genes, visualized with ggraph.

**Requirements & Reproducibility**
To run the scripts locally, you need R (>= 4.2) and the following primary libraries:    
- Core: tidyverse, ggplot2, patchwork  
- Bioconductor (Analysis): DESeq2, clusterProfiler, org.Hs.eg.db, multiMiR    
- Visualization: EnhancedVolcano, pheatmap, ggvenn, ggraph, igraph

**How to run:**

1. Clone this repository: git clone https://github.com/DariaKIL/ncRNA_heart_transplant.git
2. Open the .Rmd files in RStudio.
3. Ensure the working directory is set to the repository root.
4. Run the chunks sequentially to generate all outputs in the pictures_transpl/ folder.
